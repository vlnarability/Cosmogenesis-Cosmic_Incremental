  1662→## Design Decisions
  1663→
  1664→1. **Indirect mutual-exclusivity for ascension spires**. The `SystemDef` interface has no `exclusiveWith` field (only `TechDef` does). To make the three ascension spires mutually exclusive, I used `requiredTech` — each spire requires its respective ascension tech (`biological_ascension`, `synthetic_ascension`, `psionic_ascension`), and since those techs are already a 3-way exclusive path, exactly one spire variant is ever buildable per run. This required no changes to the SystemDef type, the store, or the UI — `requiredTech` is already wired up in `buySystem()` (store.ts:369) and the SystemsTab shows a red "Locked / Requires tech: X" badge.
  1665→
  1666→2. **Capacity-boosting systems scale upward across stages**. New capacity systems per stage: Civilization 1 (harbor), Empire 4 (railway_network, assembly_line, hospital, steel_mill), Solar 4 (space_station, lunar_base, asteroid_mining_station, habitat_ring), Galactic 3 (ftl_drive_array, wormhole_stabilizer, sentry_array). Total: 12 new capacity-boosting systems, complementing the 25 that already existed. Empire and Solar each got 4 because their primary resources (gold/production for Empire; energy/alloys for Solar) are heavily produced in those stages and need expanded caps to keep the loop flowing.
  1667→
  1668→3. **grantsAffinity density scales with stage complexity**. Cell: 13 systems with affinity (existing). My additions: Civilization 4/4 (all have affinity), Empire 7/8 (railway_network is the only one without — it's pure logistics), Solar 9/13, Galactic 14/18. Galactic has fewer-per-system because the cap is meaningful — the galactic_wonder, matter_decompressor, ftl_drive_array, wormhole_stabilizer, space_station, lunar_base, fusion_reactor, habitat_ring are concept-neutral infrastructure and don't push any single archetype.
  1669→
  1670→4. **Ascension preludes (`psionic_theory`, `synthetic_evolution`, `biological_mastery`) are NOT chained into `ascension_theory`**. They are tier-1 standalone techs that flavor the player toward a path before the formal ascension choice. A player can take all three preludes and then choose any of the three ascension paths — the preludes grant archetype drift but don't lock anything. This gives players more rope to explore the ascension theme before committing.
  1671→
  1672→5. **Galactic Governance is a SECOND 3-way exclusive path for the Galactic stage**, distinct from the ascension paths. This means a Galactic run now has TWO major mutually-exclusive choices: ascension (biological/synthetic/psionic) AND governance (federation/hegemony/alliance). The two paths are orthogonal — e.g. a player could choose Psionic Ascension + Imperial Hegemony (necroid-flavored psionic tyrant) or Synthetic Ascension + Free Worlds Alliance (arthropoid-flavored cybernetic merchant). This dramatically increases end-game replayability.
  1673→
  1674→6. **Tech tiers within each stage remain sane**. New techs honor the existing tiering convention: tier 1 (foundational, no requires), tier 2 (req a tier-1), tier 3 (req a tier-2). Galactic Governance (tier 3) requires `galactic_diplomacy` (tier 1), which requires `galactic_council` (existing tier 1) — a clean 3-step chain. dyson_sphere_tech (tier 3) requires `megastructure_construction` (existing tier 2), which requires `stellar_engineering` (existing tier 1).
  1675→
  1676→7. **All new IDs unique, all requires/exclusiveWith point at real tech IDs**. Verified via simulation script (see Verification). No conflicts with the existing 88 systems or 97 techs.
  1677→
  1678→8. **`university` (system) vs `universities` (tech)** — both existed conceptually. The Civilization tech `universities` (the technology) was already in techs.ts; my new `university` system is the physical building. Different IDs, different stages of abstraction. Same for `academy` (existing civ system) → `university` (new civ system, higher tier, more expensive). Library → Academy → University forms a 3-step science progression within the civ stage.
  1679→
  1680→9. **`steam_power` already existed as a Civilization tech** — to avoid name collision and confusion, my Empire-tier "Steam Power" themed tech is named `steam_mechanization`. Similarly `dyson_swarm` (existing Solar tech) vs my new `dyson_swarm_module` (Solar system) and `dyson_sphere_tech` (new Galactic tech that builds on the existing `dyson_sphere` system). The naming is intentional to keep IDs distinct without confusing players.

  1925→## Design Rationale
  1926→
  1927→1. **Why bump exclusive-path multipliers but not increase their costs?** Exclusive techs already cost the same as non-exclusive peers of the same tier — the opportunity cost of locking out the other 2 paths IS the cost. The reward should be at least +30% to make the choice feel meaningful. A 10-15% bonus for permanently giving up 2/3 of the branch options was not a real choice.
  1928→
  1929→2. **Why reduce production rather than increase baseCost for solar/galactic?** The baseCosts are already gated by precursor resources (e.g., solar systems cost energy + alloys, which require building up solar infrastructure first). Increasing baseCost would gate these systems behind even more grinding. Reducing production keeps the systems accessible while slowing the late-game acceleration.
  1930→
  1931→3. **Why increase upkeep rather than reduce production to fix the upkeep ratio?** The 2-3x guideline is a ratio rule. Both options work mathematically, but increasing upkeep is more thematically appropriate (e.g., a fusion reactor that consumes 0.2 alloys/s when producing 15.0 energy/s is unrealistic — it should burn through more fuel). It also creates a more interesting decision: the player has to balance resource production to feed their reactors.
  1932→
  1933→4. **Cross-stage 50% production** is already implemented in `store.ts:153` (`stageMult = sys.stage === STAGES[s.stageIndex].id ? 1 : 0.5`). Earlier-stage systems continue to produce at half-rate in later stages, keeping all 131 systems relevant throughout a run. No changes needed here.

  1935→5. **`food` is in STAGE_RESOURCES for every stage from creature onward** (creature, tribal, civilization, empire, solar, galactic). The cross-stage economy is healthy: food is produced by `grazing_grounds` (creature), `farm` (tribal), `harbor` (civ), `colony_ship` (solar), `terraformer` (solar), `ring_world` (galactic).
